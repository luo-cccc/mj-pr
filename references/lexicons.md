# Lexicons (optional phrase banks)

Use sparsely — pick what the brief needs.

## How to stack (read this first)

1. Put concrete subject and composition first unless a different order better expresses the brief.
2. Select a few useful style traits; a deliberate hybrid may need more. No fixed word count guarantees quality.
3. Combine eras when the brief calls for a hybrid; state how the elements relate.
4. Medium, light and lens words are optional descriptive tools. Film can be grain-free; an oil painting need not mention canvas.
5. Mood and palette can clarify intent; revise against the observed image.
6. `--raw` and moderate stylize values are optional photographic starting points; verify model support in [parameters.md](parameters.md).
7. Descriptive qualities can provide precise control; artist names and technical references remain valid when requested.

## Camera bodies and lenses

Camera and lens names are optional stylistic cues, not enforced optics or a guarantee of realism.

- **Bodies (color / texture signature):** `shot on ARRI Alexa` (filmic, rich color), `shot on RED` (sharp, dynamic), `Sony Venice` (low light), `Hasselblad` / `Phase One` (medium-format softness, portraits and fashion), `Leica M` (unobtrusive, reportage).
- **Focal lengths:** `14mm` / `24mm` ultra-wide — distortion, environment, cramped interiors. `35mm` — the cinematic workhorse, documentary. `50mm` — neutral, eye-level. `85mm` — compressed background, portrait. `135mm` — maximum compression, tight close-ups.
- **Aperture:** `f/1.4`–`f/1.8` shallow depth of field, heavy bokeh; `f/8`–`f/11` deep focus, product and architecture.
- **Optical artifacts:** `anamorphic lens` (horizontal flares, oval bokeh, cinemascope), `lens flare`, `vignetting`, `chromatic aberration`, `light leaks`, `dust and scratches`.

## Cinematic lighting

Source, direction and quality can make lighting instructions clearer; a short mood cue may be enough for the brief.

- **Patterns:** `three-point lighting` (key/fill/rim), `Rembrandt lighting` (triangle on the shadow cheek), `loop lighting` (soft, natural), `butterfly lighting` (key overhead center, beauty), `split lighting` (half face dark), `chiaroscuro`, `low-key lighting`, `high-key lighting`.
- **Direction:** `45 degree key light from camera left`, `rim light from behind`, `backlight`, `contre-jour`, `soft sidelight`, `top light`, `underlight`.
- **Quality:** `hard key, no fill, deep shadow camera right`, `soft diffused light`, `gradual shadow falloff`.
- **Atmosphere-dependent:** `volumetric light shafts`, `god rays`, `crepuscular rays`, `dust motes in the beam`, `fog catching light`, `light through window blinds`. Fog, dust, smoke or haze can help describe visible beams.
- **Motivated practicals:** `single tungsten desk lamp 2700K`, `neon sign magenta fill`, `TV glow on face`, `candlelight`, `overhead fluorescent 4100K`.
- **Time of day:** `golden hour` (specify `sun low behind subject`), `blue hour`, `overcast`, `harsh midday sun`, `moonlit`.
- **Kelvin (works as a hint):** 2700K tungsten · 3200K warm film · 4100K fluorescent · 5600K daylight · 6500K overcast · 9000K moonlight.

## Director and DP anchors

Director and DP names can suggest broad associations; effects vary by model and prompt.

`Denis Villeneuve` (epic scale, brutalist geometry, desolation) · `David Fincher` (low-key, precision, unease) · `Roger Deakins` (silhouette, poetic natural light) · `Wes Anderson` (symmetry, pastel palette) · `Wong Kar-wai` (saturated neon, nocturnal melancholy) · `Christopher Nolan` (imposing scale, practical realism) · `Terrence Malick` (golden hour, ethereal) · `Ridley Scott` (industrial sci-fi, emergency red, steam)

Use `directed by …` / `cinematography by …` only when you want the whole stack inherited; otherwise name one or two technical traits instead.

## Colossal scale anchors

Prefer concrete anchors over empty `giant` / `huge`:

- `taller than fifty-story towers`, `tiny traffic at street level`
- `tiny freighter ships near its ankles`, `tiny passenger jet for scale`
- `dunes reaching only its calves`, `tiny camel caravan`
- `hull larger than the metropolis below`
- Composition cues: `ultra wide view`, `aerial view`, `establishing shot` (camera terminology is also allowed)

## Negative banks (`--no` presets)

This is the canonical definition source for A1–A2, B1–B4, C1–C2, D1, E1 and F1–F6. IDs are documentation labels, never literal MJ arguments. Read [production presets](production-presets.md) and [series guidance](style-lock.md) for context.

Select and trim exclusions against intent. Single-token entries are a house readability preference, not a syntax rule. Multiword entries are allowed, but words may be interpreted independently. No bank is safe for every prompt: remove text/logo exclusions for signage, typography, branded products or visible marks; preserve intended texture, age, people and media hybrids.

### A. Optional content cleanup

| Bank | Candidate exclusions | Use and exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| A1 Unwanted marks | `text, watermark, signature, logo` | Only when these are unwanted; omit for posters, lettering and branded objects |
| A2 Unwanted visible gear | `camera, camcorder, photographer, tripod` | Only for unwanted gear in the image; keep cameras for selfies, studios or documentary crews |

### B. Medium drift corrections

| Bank | Candidate exclusions | Use and exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| B1 Photography drift | `painting, illustration, sketch, watercolor` | For unwanted painterly drift, not mixed-media photography |
| B2 Illustration drift | `photo, photorealistic, render` | For unwanted photographic/CG treatment, not dimensional illustration |
| B3 Unwanted plastic finish | `plastic, airbrushed` | For unwanted synthetic smoothness; omit for resin/BJD, plastic products or deliberate airbrushing |
| B4 Flat anime drift | `3d, photorealistic` | Only for a selected flat anime look, not every anime project |

B3 contains exactly the two candidates listed above; it does not exclude illustration, cartoon, anime or CG. Review conflicts across all banks rather than mechanically selecting a group.

### D. Cultural motif corrections

| Bank | Candidate exclusions | Exceptions |

| Bank | Candidate exclusions | Exceptions |

| E1 Unwanted oily resin finish | `greasy` | Only if the selected BJD project wants matte resin; omit for intentional oil or gloss |

### Review traps

- Do not suppress a body part to fix it. Describe the intended anatomy/pose and inspect the result; wording cannot guarantee correct hands.
- Medium exclusions can contradict hybrids or desired material response. Prefer positive descriptions before adding negatives.
- No universal skin/robe hue gap, luminance threshold, light temperature or depth-of-field requirement applies. Inspect the intended shot rather than a fixed numerical rule.
